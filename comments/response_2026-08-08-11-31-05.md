## P1 — the `eta` clamp widened a model-validity interval

**Accepted; your numbers reproduce exactly.** Running the implemented formula
for two unbounded normal margins:

```text
  log_m = -0.693   c in [1.0011e-29, 0.999999999999999]   bound [-1,        1]
  log_m =  0.000   c in [1.5820e-15, 0.999999999999999]   bound [-1,        1]
  log_m = 10.000   c in [0.99800077, 0.999999999999999]   bound [-1,  500.192]
  log_m = 20.000   c in [0.99999989, 0.999999999999999]   bound [-1, 8.97123e6]
```

`8.9712e6` at `log_m = 20`, as you reported. And the direction matters: the
clipped bound is *wider* than the theoretical one, so it is permissive — a start
at the upper dispersion clamp admits `lambda = 2` and the post-fit helper calls
it admissible, exactly as you describe. Your framing is the right one: using an
implementation clamp to widen a model-validity interval repeats the
model-versus-approximation confusion in a different form.

You were also right that the passing test established the fixture rather than
the property. At `m = 0.5` the endpoints round close enough to 0 and 1 that
`[-1, 1]` falls out regardless.

**Fixed** by deriving admissibility from the unclipped mean support. The limits
are exact in ordinary R arithmetic — `c_val(Inf, m) = 0` and `c_val(0, m) = 1`
for every finite `m > 0`, and on the Poisson branch too — so no special-casing
is needed for the infinite endpoints. The bound is now `[-1, 1]` at
`log_m = -0.693, 0, 10, 20` alike, and a test pins it across that range rather
than at one comfortable dispersion.

The *scale* clamp stays, and I want to be explicit about why that is not the
same mistake. A scale clamp cannot widen the admissible set: it only decides
whether a coefficient varies at all, and the template's floor keeps it varying,
which yields the narrower bound. Clipping `eta` caps the attainable mean and so
caps `c` away from zero, which widens. The two clamps sit on opposite sides of
the model/approximation line and are now commented as such.

## P0 — the default engine still derived its parameter space from draws

**Accepted.** You are right that this is not cross-engine polish. I accepted the
argument in the previous round and then applied it to the non-default engine
only, leaving `rpbnb(engine = "cpp")` — the default — with a parameter space that
moves with `draws`. Measured on the Rcpp engine before the change:

```text
  draws= 50  bounds=[-1.4242, 1.0995]   lambda=0.12201
  draws=200  bounds=[-1.2238, 1.1090]   lambda=0.09951
  draws=800  bounds=[-1.0796, 1.0668]   lambda=0.10556
```

Same monotone convergence toward `[-1, 1]`, same consequence.

**Fixed** by extracting `famoye_support_bounds()` into `R/famoye_core.R` and
using it in all four paths you named:

- the Rcpp objective — both the pure-R `bnbr_rp_ll_and_grad()` and the C++
  `bnbr_rp_ll_and_grad_cpp()`. The kernel needed no change: `want_grad` and
  `use_fixed` are independent flags, so the bound is computed in R from the
  current parameters and handed in through the existing fixed-bounds path while
  the gradient is still produced, and `lam`/`dlam_dz` are built from the supplied
  interval either way;
- the post-fit bound reconstruction in `fit_rpbnb()`;
- the analytic Hessian in `rpbnb_hessian.R`;
- `fit_rpbnb_tmb()`, which now calls the shared function instead of its own copy.

The engine-level regression you asked for is in `test-engine-agreement.R`: normal
support bounds independent of `draws`, and identical between `engine = "cpp"`
and `engine = "tmb"` for the same specification.

I also removed the now-dead per-draw `lambda_bounds_vec()` from the Rcpp
objective's pass-1 loop. It was being computed for every draw and no longer read.

**Impact on estimates is essentially nil**, which is worth recording because it
is not what happened on the TMB side. Bounds go to `[-1, 1]` at every draw count
while the fitted log-likelihoods are bit-identical to before
(`-1208.03029`, `-1208.31815`, `-1208.10774` at 50/200/800 draws); `lambda` moves
by about `1e-3`, which is the reparameterization of `z_dep` through a narrower
interval landing at the same interior optimum. No Rcpp baseline needed
re-capturing.

**Sites deliberately left alone**, for the record: `bnb_likelihood.R`,
`fit_bnb.R`, `simulate_bnb.R` and `simulate_rpbnb_tmb.R` still call
`lambda_bounds_vec()` directly. Those are the fixed-parameter model and the
simulators, where there is no random-coefficient integral to approximate — the
`c` values are the actual ones, so reducing over observations or over realized
coefficients *is* the support. They are not instances of the same defect.

## A defect this surfaced in my own change

Wiring the shared bound in broke eight assertions. Seven were stale test
harnesses; one was a real bug, and it is the one worth reporting.

`bnbr_rp_scores_cpp()` still reduced bounds over draws while the objective and
gradient had moved to the support bound. Per-observation scores are the OPG
covariance's ingredient and must sum to the analytic gradient; computing them
under a different interval breaks that identity and would have silently
corrupted every OPG standard error on the Rcpp engine. It is fixed to use the
same bound. `test-cpp-likelihood.R`'s "OPG per-observation scores sum to the
analytic gradient" is precisely the assertion that caught it, which is a good
argument for that test existing.

The other seven were four places that each reimplemented the old per-draw
reduction to freeze bounds for a numeric-gradient reference —
`frozen_bounds()` in `test-rpbnb-likelihood-dist.R`, `.rp_frozen_bounds()` in
`test-poisson-exact.R`, and inline blocks in `test-fit-rpbnb.R` and in that
file's mixed cross-equation test. Once the objective moved, those tests were
numerically differentiating one objective and comparing against the analytic
gradient of another. They now all delegate to the package's own
`.rp_support_bounds()` so they cannot drift again, and no reduction over draws
remains anywhere in the test tree.

One incidental improvement: those tests exist because the analytic gradient
treats the bounds as frozen and so omits a `d(bound)/d(theta)` term. For two
unbounded normal margins the support bound is the *constant* `[-1, 1]`, so that
omitted term is now exactly zero and the identity is sharper than it was under
draw-reduced bounds.

## Corrections to my previous response

- I wrote that the eta clamp was applied so "the R side and the objective share
  one parameterization at every accepted start". That is a good principle for the
  *scale* clamp and the wrong one for the mean: admissibility is a property of
  the model, and the objective's numerical window is not part of the model.
- I wrote "one support bound now serves both estimators". True of SML and Laplace
  within the TMB fitter, and not of the package's two engines, which is what the
  sentence would naturally be read to mean. It is true now.

## What is still open

1. The Gaussian atomic is not thread-safe; the public cap makes it unreachable.
2. The TMB bounds are still frozen at the starting values. With the clamp gone,
   the bound is genuinely parameter-independent for normal and lognormal
   coefficients loaded in both margins at *every* dispersion, so freezing is
   exactly harmless there rather than harmless-on-the-fixture. It still binds
   for a single varying margin and for uniform/triangular coefficients.
3. Gaussian tail cells whose NB2 CDF endpoints both reach the `safe_qnorm()`
   clamp still collapse to the `1e-300` floor.
