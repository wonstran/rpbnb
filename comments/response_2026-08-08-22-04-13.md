## P2 — the regressions did not discriminate

**Accepted on every specific.** Taking them in order:

**"reimplements the mapping locally and never calls the production guard."**
Correct, and it was the worst of the three: a test that re-derives the
arithmetic agrees with a broken guard just as happily as with a working one. The
interior logistic map is now `famoye_lam_from_z()` in `R/famoye_core.R`, called
by the guard in `fit_rpbnb()` and by the test. The test asserts both your
counterexample values — `1.523185266` from the frozen interval and
`0.3807963164` from the tautological remap — through production code.

**"comments that the intervals 'genuinely differ' but does not assert it."**
Correct. Every fit-level test now asserts
`expect_false(all.equal(fit$bounds, fit$bounds_at_optimum))` as an explicit
premise. On the uniform fixture those are `[-2.3186, 2.7647]` and
`[-2.9904, 1.6264]`. If a future change made the bound parameter-independent
there, the premise fails loudly rather than the test passing vacuously.

**"does not produce an inadmissible fit."** It does now, and constructing one
turned out to be straightforward with `.fixed`: pinning `z_lambda = 5` puts the
objective's lambda near the top of the frozen interval while the fit moves the
scale enough to narrow the interval at the optimum. Measured: frozen
`[-2.3186, 2.7647]`, optimum `[-1.7987, 1.4202]`, `lambda = 2.73066` — outside.
The test asserts the production warning fires, `lambda_admissible` is `FALSE`,
`lambda` exceeds the optimum upper bound, and the reported `lambda` is still the
objective's rather than a value remapped to look admissible.

**"uses `compute_se = FALSE`... never enters any of the OPG, analytic-Hessian,
or numeric-Hessian branches."** Correct, and this is why the previous test could
not have caught the P1 defect at all. A new test loops over
`se_method = "numeric"`, `"opg"`, `"analytic"` on the moving-bound fixture,
asserting the intervals differ, the standard errors are finite, and `summary()`
works for each.

**"the existing score identity test calls both wrappers with
`lam_bounds = NULL`."** Correct. The new test supplies the frozen interval to
both `bnbr_rp_ll_and_grad_cpp()` and `bnbr_rp_scores_cpp()`, with the evaluation
point deliberately different from the start values so that the frozen interval is
not the one the evaluation point would produce, and asserts the two differ before
comparing anything. It then asserts `colSums(scores)` equals that
frozen-objective gradient, and — the part that makes it a real guard — that
calling the score wrapper **without** `lam_bounds` breaks the identity. That
positive control is inside the test, so the test cannot silently stop
discriminating.

**"overlooks the exact path that caused the defect: a caller can omit the new
argument."** You are right and my sentence was wrong. I said scores and gradient
"take the same argument from the same variable", which is true of the one call
site I had wired and says nothing about the default. The default is silent
recomputation, which is precisely the shape of the original bug. That is now the
sensitivity control above rather than a claim in prose.

## Proof that the new tests discriminate

Because the substance of your finding is "these would have passed at `044cc9c`",
asserting that the replacements are better is not enough. I reverted the three
production changes and reran:

```text
with the broken wiring restored:
  test-fit-rpbnb.R        pass=36  FAIL=8
    >> a fit reports the lambda its own objective used, plus both intervals  (2)
    >> an escaped fit warns and is flagged inadmissible                      (3)
    >> every se_method computes covariance under the frozen interval         (3)
  test-cpp-likelihood.R   pass=14  FAIL=0

with the correct source restored:
  test-fit-rpbnb.R        pass=44  FAIL=0
  test-cpp-likelihood.R   pass=14  FAIL=0
```

`test-cpp-likelihood.R` is deliberately insensitive to the *fit-level* revert —
it calls the kernel wrappers directly — and is sensitive instead to the wrapper
dropping `lam_bounds`, which its internal positive control exercises. The two
files cover the two layers separately, which is what the previous single test
failed to do.

## What is still open

Unchanged from the previous round, and none of it is affected by this one:

1. The Gaussian atomic is not thread-safe; the public cap makes it unreachable.
2. Both engines freeze the Famoye interval at the starting values and check
   admissibility afterwards. Where the bound is parameter-independent — normal
   or lognormal loaded in both margins, at any dispersion — freezing is exact.
   Where it is not, an invalid optimum is detected and reported, not prevented;
   the escaped-fit test above is now a live demonstration of exactly that path.
3. Gaussian tail cells whose NB2 CDF endpoints both reach the `safe_qnorm()`
   clamp still collapse to the `1e-300` floor.
