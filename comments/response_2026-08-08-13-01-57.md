## P0 — the upper scale clamp widens the bound for bounded distributions

**Accepted.** I wrote that the retained scale clamp "cannot widen the admissible
set" and "only decides whether a coefficient varies at all". That is true for an
unbounded normal support and false for exactly the bounded distributions the
shared helper had just been extended to handle — which makes it a careless
generalization, since the uniform/triangular support was the thing I had argued
about two rounds earlier.

Your reproduction holds to the digit. One uniform coefficient per margin,
`log_w = 30`, loading `1e-9`, zero slopes, `m = 0.5`:

```text
  bound computed with exp(20): [-2.0362897, 2.5327946]
  actual exp(30) support:      [-1.0000000, 1.0000000]
  lambda = 2 admitted under the cap?   TRUE
  lambda = 2 admitted under the truth? FALSE
```

**Fixed** by taking your recommendation exactly: keep the underflow floor, drop
the upper cap. Every bound-side scale and dispersion is now
`exp(pmax(log_x, -20))`. The asymmetry is the point and is documented at
`famoye_support_bounds()`:

- the **floor** is conservative. It matters only for unbounded coefficients,
  where any strictly positive scale gives the same unbounded support: it stops a
  scale that underflows to 0 in R from being read as "this margin is fixed",
  which would give the *wider* fixed-margin interval. Flooring can only narrow.
- an **upper cap** is not safe, because for uniform and triangular the deviation
  support *is* `(-s, s)`; capping `s` shrinks the attainable mean range and
  widens the interval.

Verified: `.rp_support_bounds()` at `log_w = 30` now returns `[-1, 1]` and
rejects `lambda = 2`, while a normal coefficient started at `log_sd = -1000`
still returns `[-1, 1]` rather than the fixed-margin interval — so the earlier
underflow fix is intact. Both are pinned by a new regression using a bounded
distribution at an above-cap log-scale, as you asked; the existing cross-engine
test uses only normal coefficients and could not have caught this.

## P1 — the optimizer received a gradient of a different objective

**Accepted.** Reproduced on a one-margin uniform fixture: max absolute
difference between the attached analytic gradient and a central finite
difference of the *same* wrapper was `2.048`, with the `z_lambda` coordinate
agreeing to `5.7e-09` — precisely the signature you describe, since the bounds do
not depend on `z`. You were also right that the updated numeric-gradient tests
did not cover this: they freeze an interval and differentiate a separate
fixed-bounds objective, and the OPG identity is insufficient because scores and
gradient omit the same terms.

For the record, this is not something the previous round introduced: the older
draw-reduced bounds were also functions of the parameters while the gradient
treated them as constants. What the previous round did change is the *extent* —
for normal coefficients loaded in both margins the bound became the constant
`[-1, 1]`, where the omitted terms are exactly zero. It remained wherever the
bound is parameter-dependent, which is where you found it.

**Fixed** by taking your second option: the optimization objective is now
genuinely fixed-bound. `fit_rpbnb()` computes the support bound once at the
starting values and passes it to the kernels through a new `lam_bounds`
argument, so the function the optimizer evaluates has a constant interval and
the analytic gradient is its actual derivative. Left `NULL`, the kernels still
compute the bound from `par`, which is the right behaviour for one-off
evaluation and for the post-fit reconstruction.

Verified on the same parameter-dependent fixture: the discrepancy falls from
`2.048` to `5.7e-09` in the pure-R kernel and `2.6e-08` in the C++ kernel the
optimizer actually uses. A new regression differentiates that wrapper directly —
not a separate fixed-bounds reference — and first asserts that the bound really
does move with the parameters, so the test cannot quietly become vacuous.

I did not attempt your first option, including the bound derivatives. The bound
is a maximum/minimum over the corners of the attainable `c`-rectangle and over
observations, so it is piecewise-smooth with switching points; "explicit handling
of corner changes" means subgradients wherever the binding corner or the binding
observation changes, and the optimizer would then be following a direction that
is only a derivative almost everywhere. Freezing gives an exactly correct
gradient at the cost of a constraint that is documented and checked.

**The cost is the one the TMB engine already pays**, and I have made the two
engines agree rather than leaving the Rcpp side quietly different:
`fit_rpbnb()` now performs the same post-fit admissibility check, recomputing
the interval at the fitted parameters and warning when the fitted lambda has left
it. Where the bound does not depend on the parameters — normal or lognormal
coefficients loaded in both margins — freezing is exactly harmless and the check
is trivially satisfied.

## Corrections to my previous response

- "The scale clamp cannot widen the admissible set; it only decides whether a
  coefficient varies at all." False for uniform and triangular coefficients,
  whose deviation support is set by `s`. Only the *floor* has that property.
- The claim that the shared helper established one model-valid bound was
  premature: it was correct for normal fixtures and wrong for bounded supports
  at large scales.

## What is still open

1. The Gaussian atomic is not thread-safe; the public cap makes it unreachable.
2. Both engines now freeze the Famoye interval at the starting values and check
   admissibility afterwards. Where the bound is parameter-independent — normal
   or lognormal in both margins, at every dispersion — that is exact. Where it
   is not, an invalid optimum is detected and reported, not prevented. Making
   the constraint move correctly would require the bound derivatives discussed
   above, on both engines.
3. Gaussian tail cells whose NB2 CDF endpoints both reach the `safe_qnorm()`
   clamp still collapse to the `1e-300` floor.
